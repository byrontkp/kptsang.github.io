TOPIC 3 / ENDOGENOUS GROWTH

The Rebelo model

How should an economy divide its capital between producing consumption and producing more capital?

The economy in three equations

Time is continuous. We assume one unit of t represents one year, so rates are measured per year.

ConsumptionCₜ = B(φZₜ)ᵅ T¹⁻ᵅ

Capital and a fixed factor produce consumption goods.

InvestmentIₜ = A(1 − φ)Zₜ

The remaining capital produces new capital goods.

AccumulationŻₜ = Iₜ − δZₜ

New investment adds to capital; depreciation subtracts from it.

Household choice, optimal allocation, and prices

Household choice

gC = (rC − ρ) / σ

rC is the net return measured in consumption goods. ρ discounts future utility; σ measures utility curvature. With log utility, σ = 1.

For any positive variable X, gX = Ẋ/X is its instantaneous proportional growth rate.

When both sectors operate

rC = A − δ + gp
pₜ = αB(φZₜ)ᵅ⁻¹T¹⁻ᵅ / A

At zero investment

rC = ρ − σαδ
pₜ = (αCₜ/Zₜ) / [ρ + α(1−σ)δ]

The active-sector price equality is replaced by an inequality when the investment sector shuts down.

The constant optimal share

D = 1 − α(1 − σ)
φ* = [ρ − α(1−σ)(A−δ)] / AD

D is a positive abbreviation. A finite-utility interior solution requires 0 < φ* < 1.

gZ = (A − δ − ρ)/D; gC = αgZ

φ = φ*. The share stays constant along each path.

φ = 1; Iₜ = 0
gZ = −δ; gC = −αδ

The formula gives φ* ≥ 1, so the feasible optimum is the zero-investment corner.

With constant φ, gp = (α−1)gZ. A positive numerator requires ρ > α(1−σ)(A−δ).

Why this allocation? Feasibility and the transversality condition select the optimal path. The discounted marginal value of capital left in the distant future must vanish: lim e⁻ρᵗCₜ⁻σpₜZₜ = 0.
