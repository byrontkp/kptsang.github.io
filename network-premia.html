What this demonstration establishes

Production links can change the amount of consumption risk and each sector’s exposure to it. The sign and the sector ranking depend on technology, final demand and the distribution of productivity shocks. The examples illustrate a mechanism within the specified model; they are not estimates of US premia.

Equations and assumptions

There are two sectors. Terminal production is yj = zjkjαMjη, with α + η < 1. The intermediate composite is CES with elasticity σ. Household consumption is Cobb–Douglas with expenditure shares θ and 1 − θ. Capital is predetermined, there is no terminal labor or investment, and the terminal residual payoff is (1 − η)pjyj.

The primitive sourcing matrix is Ω(τ) = (1 − τ)I + τΩ*. The target off-diagonal weights are ω12 and ω21. Actual shares satisfy Wjk = ωjkpk1−σ / Σlωjlpl1−σ. Each comparison recomputes goods prices, expenditure shares and the terminal allocation.

Write L = I − ηW, A = L−1, ℓ = A′θ, δ = (1 − η)ℓ and S = diag(δ). The corrected response uses D = diag(W′δ) − W′SW, H = η(1 − σ)S−1A′D and B = L − (1 − η)H. Consumption responds with ℓ′, log returns with F = 1ℓ′ − HB−1, and log goods prices with G = −B−1 + 1ℓ′. Revenue-share feedback is retained.

The covariance premium is RP = γFΣℓ. In percentage units, the page reports 100 × RP. It is the local approximation to E[log R] − log Rf + ½Var(log R), rather than an exact finite-risk arithmetic excess return. There is no annualization. Large shock scales can make a local approximation less informative.

Productivity innovations are log zj = a + εj. The common component a is independent of ε; Corr(ε1, ε2) is adjustable. Thus Σ11 = σa² + σ1², Σ22 = σa² + σ2² and Σ12 = σa² + ρσ1σ2. All covariances refer to the same period.

Demand shares remain strictly positive so both sectors are active in the own-sector benchmark. The common capital exponent, intermediate exponent and substitution elasticity follow the existing analytical model. Their sector-specific generalization would require a new derivation.

Why are patience, the EIS and shock persistence absent?

Under the slides’ terminal Epstein–Zin specification, the state-dependent part of log M is −γ log C. Patience and the elasticity of intertemporal substitution affect date-0 saving, investment and the level of the discount factor. Conditional on capital, they do not enter the leading terminal covariance premium directly. This page therefore does not attach arbitrary effects to those parameters.

There is one uncertain terminal date. The page specifies the conditional distribution of terminal productivity directly. Persistence and future adjustment costs require an initial investment or dynamic model. Capital and reference productivity can be changed here, but their optimal initial choices are not solved.
